# DealFinder: Startup Description

## Executive summary

DealFinder is a local commerce discovery platform that helps people find useful, current offers from nearby businesses and online merchants. It brings promotions, merchant information, and location-aware discovery together in one place across a website and mobile app. Instead of relying on scattered social posts, flyers, and store-by-store searches, people can browse, compare, save, and act on offers from a single service.

The initial focus is Sri Lanka, where consumers often discover promotions through fragmented channels and merchants—especially independent and neighborhood businesses—can struggle to reach people who are actively looking to buy. DealFinder aims to make that connection easier: shoppers get a clearer way to find relevant savings, while merchants get a digital storefront for presenting offers to local customers.

## The problem

Promotions are difficult to discover when they are spread across social media pages, messaging groups, printed advertisements, and individual merchant websites. Information can be incomplete or quickly become outdated. A shopper may not know which offers are close by, whether a promotion is still active, what conditions apply, or how to contact the business or visit its online offer.

For merchants, publishing an offer across multiple channels takes time and does not guarantee that it reaches people with relevant intent. Smaller businesses may lack the budget, skills, or digital tools to maintain their own discovery and marketing systems. Even when they publish online, their offers compete with unrelated content and can be hard for customers to find later.

## The solution

DealFinder provides a shared marketplace for promotions. People can search and browse offers by category, location, merchant, and other available filters; view the details and validity of an offer; and take the next step through a merchant contact option, a visit, or an online deal link where one is provided. Location-aware discovery helps surface nearby offers, and users can save deals or follow merchants to return to them more easily.

Merchants can create and manage a business profile and publish promotions with supporting details and images. A web-based merchant experience makes offer management accessible, while the consumer website and mobile app make the same marketplace available across devices. The platform also supports bank and card promotions as a distinct offer type, helping users discover relevant savings beyond individual merchant campaigns.

## Who it serves

### Consumers

DealFinder is for people who want to stretch their budget, compare available offers, or find something nearby. This includes residents exploring their local area, families planning purchases, commuters looking for convenient options, and people searching for discounts in specific categories. The product is intended to serve both location-based shopping and online promotions.

### Merchants

The platform serves local shops, restaurants, service providers, retailers, and other businesses that want to promote products or services. It is particularly relevant to smaller merchants that need a straightforward way to publish offers and make their business discoverable without building a full marketing platform themselves.

### Offer partners

Banks and card providers can be represented through bank-offer listings, allowing users to discover promotions tied to particular payment methods or eligibility conditions.

## Product experience

A consumer can open DealFinder, explore featured and recently available promotions, search by keyword or category, and switch to nearby discovery. When location is available, the app can show offers around a selected area and display them on a map. A shopper can open an offer to review its merchant, price or discount information, dates, terms, and available contact or online actions. They can save an offer, follow a merchant, or configure notifications to keep track of relevant updates.

A merchant can maintain a profile, add business imagery, create an offer, and manage published promotions through the merchant experience. The aim is to reduce the effort between deciding to promote something and making that promotion visible to local customers.

The product is being developed for web and Android/iOS through a shared service backend. English, Sinhala, and Tamil localization is present in the mobile app, supporting a product direction suited to Sri Lanka's multilingual audience.

## Focused first experience

Although the platform can support several kinds of offers and audiences, the consumer experience should make the main job immediately clear: find a worthwhile offer and decide what to do next. The recommended primary navigation is:

- **Discover:** relevant, popular, featured, and newly available offers.
- **Nearby:** offers around the user's current or selected area, with a map and a list view.
- **Categories:** focused browsing across areas such as food, shopping, electronics, wellness, and travel.
- **Bank Offers:** promotions organized around participating banks, cards, and their eligibility terms.
- **Saved:** deals the user wants to revisit.
- **Merchant Portal:** a separate workspace for publishing and managing business profiles and promotions.

This keeps the architecture broad while giving consumers a small number of understandable starting points. The exact navigation can evolve through usage data and user feedback.

## Prioritized product roadmap

These are product improvements to prioritize as the marketplace grows. Some supporting capabilities may already exist in parts of the product; the roadmap describes the quality and completeness of the experience to aim for.

| Priority | Improvement | Why it matters | Product direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Deal verification and freshness | Trust is the foundation of a deal marketplace. Expired, misleading, or incomplete offers quickly discourage repeat use. | Make validity dates, eligibility, and redemption terms clear; provide merchant confirmation and user reporting; detect and remove expired offers; and visibly distinguish verified information from unverified details. |
| High | “Best Value Near You” | Gives users a reason to return and makes location-based discovery useful beyond a map of pins. | Rank nearby offers using transparent factors such as discount or value, distance, freshness, relevance, and availability. Explain the ranking in plain language and avoid presenting paid placement as an organic recommendation. |
| High | Better offer comparison | Helps users make purchase decisions instead of simply browsing promotions. | Let users compare key details such as final price, original price where reliable, savings, distance, expiry, conditions, and online or in-store availability. |
| High | Bank and card eligibility filters | Bank promotions are only useful when people can quickly tell whether they qualify. | Filter by bank, card network or card type, payment method, offer period, merchant/category, and relevant eligibility conditions; show caps and exclusions prominently. |
| High | Merchant performance analytics | Gives merchants a reason to stay active and helps them improve their offers. | Provide understandable measures such as views, saves, contact or link actions, and offer performance over time, with privacy-conscious reporting and clear metric definitions. |
| Later | Product price comparison | Expands DealFinder from promotion discovery into everyday savings decisions. | Explore comparable product listings and price histories only when there is enough reliable, standardized product and price data to keep comparisons accurate. |
| Later | Personalized savings assistant | Could build long-term differentiation by helping people find offers that match their interests and habits. | Begin with explicit user preferences and explainable recommendations. Add more advanced assistance only when data quality, consent, and user controls are strong. |

The near-term sequence should start with offer trust and freshness, then improve nearby value ranking, comparison, and bank-offer eligibility. Merchant analytics follows as the supply side matures. Price comparison and personalized assistance are longer-term bets because they depend on broader, more consistent data and sustained usage.

## Value proposition

**For consumers:** discover offers from multiple businesses in one place, find options near a chosen location, understand the offer before acting, and keep useful deals accessible.

**For merchants:** publish promotions through a dedicated discovery channel, present business information and imagery alongside the offer, and reach people browsing by location or category.

**For the local marketplace:** improve the connection between available local offers and people seeking them, while giving smaller businesses a more accessible route to online visibility.

## What makes the concept distinct

DealFinder combines local discovery with a promotions marketplace. The product is designed to connect an offer to the practical information needed to use it: who is providing it, where it can be used, when it is valid, and how to contact the merchant or open the online offer. Location-aware browsing, merchant profiles, saved offers, notifications, and bank offers complement the core promotion listing.

The opportunity is not simply to publish more advertisements. It is to make offers easier to find and evaluate at the moment a person is considering a purchase, and easier for a merchant to publish without managing a separate campaign across many channels.

## Business model direction

The product can explore several revenue streams as the marketplace develops. These are business-model options, not claims that every option is currently active:

- Paid promotion or boosted placement for merchants that want additional visibility.
- Merchant subscription plans for enhanced profile, offer-management, or performance-reporting tools.
- Campaign packages for businesses that need time-limited reach or seasonal promotion.
- Partnerships with banks, card providers, or other organizations that want to distribute eligible offers.
- Aggregate marketplace insights for participating businesses, provided reporting protects individual users' privacy.

A sensible early approach is to first establish reliable offer quality, repeat consumer usage, and merchant retention. Monetization can then be tested in ways that are clearly labeled and preserve trust in the discovery experience.

## Early growth strategy

DealFinder can begin with focused geographic areas and a manageable set of high-interest categories. Recruiting a diverse group of local merchants gives consumers a reason to return; useful consumer activity gives merchants a reason to keep their offers current. Partnerships with business associations, local communities, and payment providers may help seed both sides of the marketplace.

Early operating priorities should include making merchant onboarding simple, helping merchants publish complete and current offers, providing clear expiry and eligibility details, and collecting feedback from both shoppers and businesses. Local-language content and support can help build trust and adoption in the initial market.

## Key success measures

The startup should track whether the marketplace is useful and sustainable for both sides. Potential measures include:

- Active merchants and the share that publish offers regularly.
- Number and freshness of live offers, including the rate of expired or incomplete listings.
- Consumer activation and repeat use over time.
- Searches that lead to offer views, saves, merchant contact, directions, or online link visits.
- Nearby discovery usage and successful map/location experiences.
- Merchant retention and satisfaction, plus the time required to publish an offer.
- Cost of acquiring consumers and merchants compared with their ongoing activity.

These measures should be defined precisely before being used for investor or public claims.

## Challenges to solve

A promotions marketplace depends on accurate, current information. DealFinder will need clear merchant publishing flows, appropriate review and reporting mechanisms, visible dates and conditions, and a reliable process for removing expired or misleading offers. It must also earn location permission through clear user value and provide a useful experience when location is unavailable.

The business must attract both consumers and merchants, maintain enough offer density in each area, and distinguish sponsored placements from organic discovery. It should protect account and location data, ensure images and links are served reliably, and make the service accessible across a range of devices and network conditions.

## Vision

DealFinder's long-term vision is to become a trusted everyday starting point for discovering local value: a place where people can find relevant offers from nearby businesses and online providers, and where merchants of different sizes can reach customers with timely, understandable promotions.

**In one sentence:** DealFinder helps people discover and act on local and online offers, while giving merchants a simple way to put their promotions in front of interested customers.

## Short pitch

DealFinder is a web and mobile marketplace for discovering local and online promotions. Consumers can search by category, explore nearby offers, compare details, save deals, follow merchants, and act through contact or online links. Merchants get a simple way to publish business profiles and promotions, while bank offers and location-aware discovery broaden the value of the marketplace. DealFinder is starting with Sri Lanka and is designed to make fragmented promotions easier to find, understand, and use.
